<div class="essay-question">When the New York Police Department (NYPD) was established in 1845, Mayor Isaac Varian expressed the hope that the city's peace officers would be "but a part of the citizens" rather than outsiders. To that end, each ward had its own police station, while policemen were required to live in the precinct. Local control was a political necessity of the time, but with the department established, localism began a long decline. The residency requirement was never strictly enforced, but at first, two-thirds to three-fourths of the police lived in the ward in which they worked.
